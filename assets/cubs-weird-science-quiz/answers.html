Weird but True Science: Leader answers

10 questions, 1 point each. Teams write A, B or C; swap papers before marking.

  1. How many hearts does an octopus have?

    C: Three. An octopus has three hearts.

  2. What do butterflies use to taste?

    A: Their feet. Butterflies have taste sensors on their feet.

  3. What shape is wombat poo?

    B: Cube-shaped. Wombats produce cube-shaped droppings.

  4. What can an axolotl regrow?

    A: Lost limbs. Axolotls can regenerate limbs.

  5. How can dolphins sleep while still surfacing to breathe?

    B: One brain hemisphere rests at a time. Dolphins can rest one brain hemisphere at a time; one eye may remain open.

  6. What colour is a polar bear's skin?

    C: Black. Polar bears have black skin beneath their fur.

  7. How can some aquatic turtles take in oxygen underwater?

    A: Through specialised tissue near their rear end. Some turtles absorb dissolved oxygen through specialised cloacal tissue.

  8. Which animal has fingerprints surprisingly similar to ours?

    A: Koala. Koalas have fingerprints that resemble human prints.

  9. How many eyelids are commonly described for a camel's eye?

    C: Three. Camels have upper and lower eyelids plus a protective third eyelid.

  10. What can a wood frog survive during winter?

    A: Being partly frozen. Wood frogs can survive partial freezing using protective physiological adaptations.

Leader note: Camel eyelids includes the third nictitating membrane. Turtle oxygen absorption applies only to certain species. Review facts and suitability before delivery.
