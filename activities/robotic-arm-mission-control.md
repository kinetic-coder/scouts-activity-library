# Robot Arm Relay Challenge

## Purpose and observable objectives
Beavers use a grabber carefully to move objects as a team, practising control and explaining that astronauts use robotic tools such as Canadarm2 on the ISS.

## Kit
- Picker/grabber tools, ideally one per Beaver or enough to share fairly.
- Six lightweight plastic balls large enough not to pose a choking hazard.
- Two buckets: one start bucket with all balls and one empty destination bucket.
- Clean, comfortable non-latex gloves for every Beaver.
- Timer, pen and [printable score sheet](#printable-score-sheet).

**Low-kit alternative:** use clean kitchen tongs and rolled socks/beanbags large enough to handle safely. If a Beaver cannot use gloves or a grabber, offer timer, scorekeeper, caller or an adapted transfer role.

## Setup
No table is required. Put buckets a safe walking distance apart with a clear route. Inspect grabbers for dirt, sharp edges and pinch points. Write each team's chosen name on the score sheet before it arrives.

## Procedure (11 minutes)
1. **Explain and demonstrate — 1–2 minutes.** Demonstrate picking up one ball while wearing gloves. Explain that space robotic arms move equipment carefully; accuracy matters as well as speed.
2. **Relay — up to 8–9 minutes.** One Beaver picks up one ball, walks to the destination bucket and drops it in. The next Beaver then takes a turn. Beavers walk; they do not run, throw or kick balls.
3. **Dropped-ball rule.** If a ball is dropped, return that ball to the original bucket and restart that turn.
4. **Record and repeat.** Stop the timer when every ball is in the destination bucket. Record the time, reset, and repeat complete attempts while time allows. The fastest completed attempt is the score; do not begin an attempt that cannot finish and be tidied safely.
5. **Finish — final minute.** Put tools down, record the best score and arrange handwashing with soap and water. If this cannot happen safely inside the rotation, the session leader must supervise it immediately after the station.

## Accessibility and adaptations
Use a shorter route, a larger/lighter object or a partner as needed. A Beaver can hold the bucket steady, time, record, encourage, or take a stationary transfer role. Celebrate careful teamwork rather than ranking individuals.

## Safety controls (draft for leader review)
Keep the route clear and use soft, non-chokable objects. Inspect and clean tools; no running; keep fingers clear of grabber mechanisms. Confirm handwashing access and supervision. The responsible leader must review this draft with group risk procedures and individual adjustments before use.

## Tidy and reset
Return balls to the start bucket, clean/store tools and gloves, and retain the score sheet for the session recap.
