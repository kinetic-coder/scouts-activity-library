# Earth and Solar System Mission

## Purpose
Explore the Solar System through a short, cooperative ordering challenge. Beavers identify the Sun, the eight planets and Pluto, then share what makes each world different.

## Observable objectives
- Put the eight planets in the correct order away from the Sun.
- Recognise that the Sun is a star and Pluto is a dwarf planet.
- Share or listen to one fact about a Solar System object.

## Kit
- 1 table, large enough to lay out the cut-outs in a line.
- 10 labelled Solar System cut-outs: Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto. The name on each cut-out should be easy to read.
- 1 pair of clean gloves, used as an astronaut role prop.
- 1 printed copy of [Solar System order](#printable-solar-system-order) below.
- 1 printed copy of [Solar System fact cards](#printable-solar-system-fact-cards) below.

**Low-kit alternative:** write the ten names on large slips of paper instead of using cut-outs. If gloves are unavailable, nominate an “astronaut” without the prop.

## Setup
1. Put the table where every Beaver can reach it safely. Put the named cut-outs in a mixed-up pile at one end.
2. Print the order sheet and fact cards below. Keep the order sheet hidden until the check; cut apart the fact cards if leaders or Beavers will each hold one.
3. Explain that the Sun is a star. There are eight planets; Pluto comes after Neptune but is a **dwarf planet**, not a ninth planet.

## Instructions
1. Give one Beaver the gloves and call them the astronaut. They choose a cut-out, read the name (or ask for help), and place it on the table. Pass the gloves to the next astronaut after each turn.
2. As a team, arrange the objects in order away from the Sun. Use spoken clues and give everyone a chance to contribute. Avoid a speed race.
3. Reveal the printable order sheet and check together: Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto.
4. Read one or more relevant fact-card bullets as each cut-out is checked. Ask the team to choose one fact they found surprising.
5. Recap: the Sun is a star, Earth is our home planet, and Pluto is a dwarf planet. Return the cut-outs, gloves and papers to the kit box.

## Discussion prompts
- Which object gives light and heat to the Solar System?
- Which planet is our home? What helps living things survive here?
- Which worlds are rocky, and which are giant worlds made mostly of gas or ice?
- Why do we call Pluto a dwarf planet?

## Accessibility and adaptations
- Pair confident readers with anyone who would like help, and read labels and fact cards aloud.
- Let a Beaver point, give a verbal direction, hold a fact card, or be the order checker instead of handling a cut-out.
- Use a high-contrast, large-print order sheet and large cut-outs; keep the table accessible from a seated position.
- Reduce to Sun, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and Pluto first, then add the remaining planets if the group needs a simpler challenge.

## Tidy and reset
Count all ten cut-outs, stack them together, return the gloves to the kit box, and keep the printed sheets with this activity for the next leader.

## Optional variations
- Give each Beaver one fact card and ask them to find the matching cut-out before arranging the line.
- Use the cut-outs as a floor line only where there is a clear, supervised space and no trip risk; do not ask Beavers to run between them.

## Printable Solar System order

Print this section for the table check.

**Away from the Sun:**

1. Sun *(a star)*
2. Mercury
3. Venus
4. Earth
5. Mars
6. Jupiter
7. Saturn
8. Uranus
9. Neptune
10. Pluto *(a dwarf planet)*

There are eight planets: Mercury through Neptune. Pluto is beyond Neptune and is a dwarf planet.

## Printable Solar System fact cards

Print this section, or cut the headings and bullets into cards. Use the facts as prompts rather than asking Beavers to memorise all of them.

### Sun — star
- The Sun is the star at the centre of our Solar System.
- It gives Earth light and heat.
- The planets travel around the Sun.

### Mercury — planet 1
- Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun.
- It is the smallest planet in our Solar System.
- It is a rocky planet with lots of craters.

### Venus — planet 2
- Venus is the second planet from the Sun.
- It is covered by thick clouds.
- It is about the same size as Earth.

### Earth — planet 3
- Earth is the third planet from the Sun.
- It is our home planet.
- Earth has oceans of liquid water and air that living things can use.

### Mars — planet 4
- Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun.
- It is often called the Red Planet because of rusty material in its soil.
- Mars is a rocky planet.

### Jupiter — planet 5
- Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun.
- It is the biggest planet in our Solar System.
- Jupiter is a gas giant, so it does not have a solid surface to stand on.

### Saturn — planet 6
- Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun.
- It is the second-largest planet in our Solar System.
- Saturn has bright rings made mostly of ice and rock pieces.

### Uranus — planet 7
- Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun.
- It is an ice giant.
- Uranus spins on its side compared with most planets.

### Neptune — planet 8
- Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun.
- It is an ice giant.
- Neptune has very fast winds.

### Pluto — dwarf planet
- Pluto is a dwarf planet beyond Neptune.
- It is smaller than Earth’s Moon.
- Pluto takes about 248 Earth years to travel once around the Sun.
